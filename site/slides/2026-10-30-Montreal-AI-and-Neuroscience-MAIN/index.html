A brief history of deep active computer vision

where to look, learned by reinforcement learning

by the entropy of its decoder's attention

  • RAM, Mnih et al. 2014 (Mnih: deep Q-learning): where to look learned by RL.
  • DRAM, Ba et al. 2015: house numbers (SVHN).
  • Saccader, Elsayed et al. 2019: 75% on ImageNet-1k with a separate NASNet classifier reading its glimpses (70% alone); a pretraining step to stabilize learning.
  • GFNet, Wang et al. 2020: glance, then focus; efficiency; classification only, fixed zoom.
  • AME, Pardyl et al. 2023: segmentation; next glimpse from the entropy of its decoder's attention maps.
  • AdaGlimpse, Pardyl et al. 2024: chooses position and zoom.
  • AdaptiveNN, Wang et al. 2025: classification, fixed zoom.
  • All except AME learn where to look with RL.
  • CanViT, NeurIPS 2026: no learned policy.

The wide gap between passive and active computer vision

  • ImageNet-1k: a label per image, 1,000 classes. English springer.
  • ADE20K: a class per pixel, 150 classes, wall and floor included.
  • Harder for an active model: you need what is everywhere, including where you did not look closely. Most active models cannot output it at all (paper).
  • mIoU: per class, overlap of predicted and true regions over their union; averaged over classes.
  • Lines: passive, base size, trained end to end; frozen self-supervised features + linear layer.
  • Active on ADE20K: AME 27.6, AdaGlimpse 25.7.
  • Paper: active models have struggled to match passive ones.

No policy can make up for a poor observer. A general-purpose observer lets you use any policy.

  • Paper: instantaneous vision, memory, action selection. Observer: the first two. Policy: the third.
  • I made the same mistake as everybody else: I focused on choosing where to look.
  • Random viewpoints against the paper's own choice: GFNet, ImageNet-1k, 74.5 vs 75.9; AME, SUN360, 72.4 vs 73.4.
  • It doesn't matter what policy I have if I don't have a good observer.
  • With a good observer, the policy matters most when glimpses are few: after one glimpse, C2F 76.8 vs R-IID 47.7 top-1 (#policy-accuracy).
  • "Any policy": the policies tested.

CanViT in action

  • Viewpoint: center and scale (the crop's half side). Here random (R-IID): CanViT does not choose them.
  • Glimpse: 128 × 128 px at any scale; nothing outside it. t: the glimpse's index, from 0.
  • A world of difference between seeing something blurry and seeing nothing at all.
  • Canvas colors: PCA, as for DINOv3.
  • Readout: a linear layer, at any time.
  • DINOv3 on the whole scene at 128 px, the pixels of one glimpse: 8 × 8 patches. Pixel accuracy, street 72%, against CanViT's 88% after 21 glimpses; shop 70 against 78; ferry 41 against 71.
  • Same input on ADE20K (paper): DINOv3 ViT-B at 128 px 28.8 mIoU; CanViT-B, one full-scene glimpse, 29.3 to 39.6 by canvas size.

Canvas Vision Transformer architecture

  • ViT-B backbone, randomly initialized, processes each glimpse.
  • Canvas: 32 × 32 in pretraining, 64 × 64 here, set at inference.
  • Read: the glimpse queries the canvas; what earlier glimpses wrote shapes how this one is processed: top-down recurrent feedback. Write: the canvas queries the glimpse.
  • Canvas side: no learned projections, only LayerNorm and RoPE. Cheap to read and write, so the canvas can be large.
  • Backbone retinotopic, canvas spatiotopic; scene-relative RoPE binds them.
  • Shown: the canvas after each write. Paper, canvas evolution figure: the write residuals go from the glimpse's patch grid, to the objects in it, to beyond the glimpse.
  • I put the intelligence on the Vision Transformer side, and made the memory dumb, but not too dumb.
  • Next glimpse: new glimpse patches; the canvas and the CLS token carry over.

Viewing policies

  • R-IID, F-IID: pretraining's two rollouts. The others were never seen in pretraining.
  • C2F: quadtree, coarse to fine, random order within a level. F2C: C2F reversed: the same viewpoints, the opposite order.
  • EG-C2F: C2F, most uncertain tile first (next slide). No RL.
  • RFS: the full scene, repeated: more processing, no new input.

Uncertainty-based viewpoint selection

  • A distribution over 150 classes per canvas cell.
  • Building cell: building 0.997. Car cell: car 0.27, building 0.27, road 0.12; 3.2 bits (max 7.2).
  • EG-C2F: within each quadtree level, the unvisited tile with the highest mean entropy. No RL.
  • Metacognition, in this narrow sense: the model's own uncertainty about what is where. Over ADE20K validation, the surest tenth of canvas cells is 99% right, the least sure 37%; AUROC 0.84 (Backup, #calibration).
  • Renninger, Verghese & Coughlan 2007 (J. Vis.): in a shape-learning task, a local rule, look where uncertainty is highest, predicts people's next fixations at least as well as maximizing information. EG-C2F's rule.

Benchmark results on ADE20K and ImageNet-1k

  • ImageNet-1k: 81.1 frozen, 84.5 fine-tuned; AdaptiveNN 82.2, trained end to end (+2.3).
  • ADE20K: 45.9 frozen (C2F, 64 × 64 canvas); AME 27.6.
  • Paper: CanViT-B outperforms prior active vision models on both by a wide margin.
  • ImageNet-1k, the dashed line's last step: frozen DINOv3 ViT-B, CanViT-B's teacher, with the paper's probe: 85.0.
  • DINOv3 ViT-B with our linear probe: 47.2. Dashed line: 51.8, DINOv3's own protocol; what explains the gap was not measured.

Accuracy by viewing policy

  • ADE20K, 21 glimpses, 64 × 64 canvas: C2F 45.9, F-IID 44.9, R-IID 43.1, F2C 42.9.
  • C2F and F2C: same viewpoints by the end, different order.
  • Keyword: coarse-to-fine: people categorize coarse-to-fine sequences of the same scene faster than fine-to-coarse ones (Kauffmann et al. 2015; filtered images, not viewpoints).
  • ImageNet-1k: far apart after one glimpse (C2F 76.8, F2C 32.2 top-1), within 1.3 points after 21 (RFS aside).
  • RFS: recurrent processing alone helps at first; without new viewpoints, accuracy then declines (paper).

Backup

Policy agnosticism

  • Random locations and zoom levels; scale down to 0.05 (0.25% of the area); small glimpses more likely.
  • Random rollout length: 4 glimpses on average.
  • Each scene in a batch: an R-IID and an F-IID rollout; F-IID starts with the full scene.
  • C2F, F2C, EG-C2F, RFS: inference only.
  • 13.2M ImageNet-21k scenes, about 1 billion glimpses, 166 hours on a single H100.

A persistent, evolving understanding of the scene

  • Glimpses: a shop sign, people, a bicycle. Maps: p(class) per canvas cell.
  • Kept: the state carried across glimpses. Reset: a fresh state (canvas and CLS) before each glimpse.
  • If you reset the memory, unless you get a good look at something, the representation is bad. With the memory retained, it’s okay: you got a good look before.
  • 6,218 three-object sequences in ADE20K validation, glimpses left to right; after the third glimpse, mean p over the first object: 0.41 kept, 0.04 reset; over the second: 0.45, 0.04.
  • Keyword: trans-saccadic integration.

Canvas Attention

  • Asymmetric: QKVO projections on the glimpse side only; canvas side: LayerNorm, RoPE and residual addition.
  • Read/write pair, 64 × 64 canvas: 2.8 GFLOPs; with canvas-side projections 37.3.
  • Previous dense models (AME, AdaGlimpse): a cheap encoder and a massive decoder.
